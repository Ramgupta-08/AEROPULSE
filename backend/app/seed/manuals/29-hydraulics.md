# Chapter 29 — Hydraulic Power

## GMM 29-10 Hydraulic pressure low or dropping after landing
Symptoms: system pressure decays after landing or during taxi, pressure caution light, fluid seepage on one side.
Likely causes, most to least probable:
1. Dynamic seal leak at an actuator or pump outlet (check seal batch and hours since installation).
2. Accumulator pre-charge low — pressure falls quickly when demand stops.
3. Hydraulic pump wear — high case-drain flow, pressure ripple.
4. Pressure transmitter drift — indication fault with normal system behaviour.
Recommended checks:
- Inspect actuator rod seals and pump outlet fittings on the affected side for wetness; clean and run the system for 5 minutes, then re-inspect.
- Measure accumulator pre-charge with system depressurised (limit: ±5 % of placard).
- Measure pump case-drain flow at ground idle; above limit indicates pump wear.
- Compare indicated pressure with a calibrated gauge at the ground test point.
Rectification: replace the seal kit (part "Hydraulic seal kit"), bleed and service the system, perform leak check at full pressure for 10 minutes.

## GMM 29-12 Seal batch traceability
Seal kits are batch-controlled. When two or more premature seal failures (below 25 % of expected life) share a batch number,
raise a quality report, quarantine remaining stock of that batch at all bases and plan replacement on aircraft that carry it.

## GMM 29-20 Hydraulic pump replacement
Depressurise system, cap lines, remove pump. Fit new pump with new seals, prime case with clean fluid before first run.
Flush system filter, replace filter element, ground run and check case-drain flow. Duration: 6–8 man-hours.

## GMM 29-30 Fluid contamination
Dark fluid, metallic particles on filter or burnt odour indicates pump distress. Take a fluid sample, replace filter elements,
flush the affected system and inspect pump before return to service.
